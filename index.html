ABOUT US

From Loom to Legacy:
The Story of Mina Tricots Before Mina Tricots became a brand, it was simply our family's way of surviving. Deep in the heart of the Atlas Mountains, in a small Amazigh village where resources were scarce but heritage ran deep, our family had only two things to rely on: unyielding resilience and a modest wooden loom passed down through generations. Money was tight, winter winds were unforgiving, and opportunities were hard to come by. Driven by necessity, our parents began crafting clothing right from our home. Every piece was made by hand—spun from raw wool, woven with patient care, and stitched with purpose. We didn't just make garments to stay warm; we made them to live. When we created our very first line of shirts, we didn't have luxury fabrics or state-of-the-art machinery. What we had was an absolute commitment to quality, respect for our roots, and the vibrant patterns of our Amazigh culture. We began selling them locally, trading garment by garment, shirt by shirt. Word slowly spread. People didn't just fall in love with the durability and comfort of our shirts—they felt the heart and soul woven into every seam Through relentless hard work, long nights, and the support of a community that believed in us, what started as a desperate effort to support our home grew into a thriving business. Today, Mina Tricots stands as a testament to where we come from and how far perseverance can take you. We still honor those humble beginnings in everything we design. When you wear Mina Tricots, you aren't just wearing a shirt—you are wearing a story of heritage, survival, and hard-earned success.
